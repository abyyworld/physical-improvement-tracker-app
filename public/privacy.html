Arise

Privacy policy

Last updated: 10 October 2026

Arise is a free app for daily quests toward your goals, with an optional workout plan and an AI coach. This page says what Arise keeps, where it's kept, and who can see it.

The short version

  • Everything you put in Arise is saved on your device.
  • An account is optional. With one, a copy is kept in the cloud so your devices stay in step. With a recovery code (the default), it's end-to-end encrypted: nobody else can read it, including the people who run Arise. If you choose Email reset instead, the cloud keeps a copy of the key, so Google and we could read it.
  • The AI coach is private by default. Only if you pick the free AI or your own AI service, and say yes, can another company read what the coach sends.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking. Your data is never sold.

Who runs Arise

Arise is a project on GitHub, run by abyyworld. Its code is public at github.com/abyyworld/physical-improvement-tracker-app, so anyone can check what this page says. "We" on this page means the people who run Arise.

This page covers the website (abyyworld.github.io/physical-improvement-tracker-app), the app you install from it on a phone or computer, and the iPhone app.

What's kept on your device

Everything you put in Arise is saved in the app's storage on your device: your goals, quests and ticks, measures and milestones, workouts, the daily log, weigh-ins and body measurements, your answers from the intro, your settings, and your chats and messages with the AI coach.

It isn't encrypted on the device itself. Anyone who can unlock your device and open Arise can see it.

Without an account, none of this leaves your device, unless you send it somewhere yourself: by saving a backup, or by using your own AI service (see The AI coach).

  • Backups. Settings, Backup saves everything to a file. The file is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.
  • Your AI key, if you add one, stays on that device. It's never synced and never put in backups.
  • The iPhone app also keeps a copy of your data in the Files app (On My iPhone, Arise), so it can put your data back if iOS ever clears the app's storage. Deleting the app deletes it. Like any app's data, it can be part of your iPhone's own backups (iCloud or a computer).
  • Reminders. The iPhone app's notifications are set up on the phone itself; no server sends them. The web app's notifications come from a small Arise server, which never knows what they say (see Reminders on the web). For them, the app keeps the next two weeks of reminder texts on your device. "Add reminders to my calendar" saves a calendar file on your device. If you add it to a calendar that syncs (Google, iCloud and so on), that calendar service keeps those events.

Accounts and sync

An account is optional. It keeps the same history on every device where you sign in. You can make one with an email address, or with no email at all: then you get an account code (like ARISE-7KQ2-9XMP-4HTR-3C8D) to sign in with. Accounts and the cloud copy are run on Google Firebase. Without an account, Arise doesn't contact Firebase at all.

Two kinds of email account

When you make an account with an email, you choose how you get back in if you forget your password. That also decides who else could read your synced data.

  • Recovery code (most private). The default, and the only kind for a no-email account. Your synced data is end-to-end encrypted: nobody else can read it, including us. If you forget your password, your recovery code is the only way back in.
  • Email reset (easier). If you forget your password, Firebase emails you a link to set a new one. To make that work, your account keeps a copy of the key to your data in the cloud. Only your account can fetch it, when it's signed in. But Google (who host it) and we could read your data if we chose to, or if someone made us. You get a recovery code too, as a second way back in.

You can switch any time in Settings, Account, More. Switching to Recovery code deletes the copy of the key from the cloud. The key itself stays the same, so this can't undo a copy someone may have taken while it was there.

Encrypted on your device

Before your data leaves your device, it's encrypted there (AES-256-GCM) with a random key. The cloud only ever holds the encrypted copy, and the database rules refuse anything that isn't encrypted. With a recovery code account, only your devices have the key, so nobody else can read it: not the people who run Arise, not Google, and not anyone who asks either of them for it. With an Email reset account, the cloud also keeps a copy of the key, as above.

What syncs: everything listed above, except your AI key, which AI service you use, the notification switch (and the web app's reminder times), AI usage counts and a workout in progress. Those stay on each device.

Your password

Your password never leaves your device. The app stretches it on your device (PBKDF2, 600,000 rounds) into two separate keys. One is what the sign-in service gets in place of your password. The other unlocks the key to your data. The sign-in service can't turn what it gets back into either.

With a recovery code account, that's why nobody can reset your password for you. If you forget it, your recovery code is the only way back in.

One exception, with Email reset only: after your password was set from a reset email, the sign-in service has that password itself. So when the usual sign-in fails, the app also sends your password as you typed it. Once that works, the app switches the sign-in service back to the value made from it.

Your recovery code

When you make an account, Arise shows you a recovery code once. Keep it somewhere safe and private. It also unlocks your data, so with it you can set a new password. We never see it and can't send it to you again. With a recovery code account, if you lose both your password and your recovery code, nobody can open your cloud copy, including us. The data on your devices stays.

For the recovery code to work, a small record is kept in the cloud under your sign-in email (or account code). It's locked with your recovery code, so it's no use to anyone without it. It also says whether the account uses Email reset, so the app knows before you sign in. Someone who knows your sign-in email could find out that it has an Arise account, and which kind.

What the server can still see

  • Your sign-in email. With a no-email account, only your account code.
  • When your devices sync, and roughly how much data you have (the size of the encrypted copy).
  • The IP address your device connects from, as with any website.

Not what any of your data says, unless your account uses Email reset (see above).

Arise doesn't send you emails. The only one you can get is a password reset link, which Firebase sends. The app only offers one for an account that uses Email reset. But Firebase sends one for any email account if someone asks it to, so you could get one you didn't ask for. You can ignore it. With a recovery code account, a password set that way can't open your data without your recovery code.

The AI coach

The AI coach is optional. Everything else in Arise works without it. To help you, it's given your profile and intro answers, your goals and quests and how they've gone, your workouts, weigh-ins and daily log, and what you type to it. You pick where it runs in Settings, AI coach.

Private AI

An open AI model running in secure enclaves at Tinfoil. Before anything is sent, the app checks that the enclave is running the exact published code on genuine secure hardware, then encrypts what it sends so only that enclave can read it. The answer comes back encrypted too.

Requests pass through a small Arise service on Cloudflare, which holds Arise's Tinfoil key and limits how much each account can use. It can't read what you ask or what the AI answers. It sees which account asked, from which IP address, when, and how big the request was. To know which account asked, it gets your sign-in token, which includes your sign-in email (or account code). It keeps no logs. All it keeps is one counter per account for the day's allowance, deleted at midnight UTC. Tinfoil sees that a request came from Arise, when, and how big it was. Your device also fetches the enclave's proof from Tinfoil to check it, so Tinfoil sees your IP address then.

The private AI needs an account.

On this device

Chrome's built-in AI model on laptops and desktops (Chrome 148 or later). What you ask never leaves your computer.

Free AI (less private)

The same kind of open AI model, run by Cloudflare Workers AI on Cloudflare's servers. It's free for you, with a daily limit. It's less private than the private AI. The request is protected on its way there, but there's no sealed enclave for the app to check: Cloudflare's servers read what the coach sends in order to answer it. Cloudflare says it doesn't use what's sent to Workers AI to train AI models. The model runs on Cloudflare's own servers, so the company that made it doesn't get your data. What else Cloudflare keeps, and for how long, is covered by Cloudflare's privacy policy. We can't check that the way the app checks the private AI's enclave.

Requests go through the same small Arise service on Cloudflare as the private AI. Here it isn't encrypted to an enclave, so that service handles the request to pass it on: it only passes on the parts the AI needs, and keeps none of it. Like for the private AI, it sees which account asked (with your sign-in email or account code), from which IP address, when, and how big the request was. It keeps no logs. All it keeps is a separate counter per account for the free AI's daily allowance, deleted at midnight UTC.

The free AI needs an account. The app never picks it by itself: only after you pick it and say yes to it being less private.

Your own AI service

You can connect your own key from Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google), OpenAI, OpenRouter, Groq, or another service you add. This is not private. The app sends your data straight from your device to that company, which can read it and handles it under its own privacy policy. We don't see it. The app only does this after you pick that service and say yes to it.

The daily message

When the AI coach is set up, it writes you a personal message once a day when you open the Today screen, using the same data. It's on unless you turn it off. With the free AI or your own AI service, that means your data goes to Cloudflare or that company once a day, even if you don't tap anything else. Turn it off in Settings, AI coach, Daily System message.

Reminders on the web

Notifications are optional, and off until you turn them on in Settings, Reminders, on each device. The iPhone app sets up its own on the phone. A web app can't, so for the website and the app you install from it, a small Arise server on Cloudflare sends each reminder at its time. You don't need an account for it.

For each device that turns them on, the reminders server keeps:

  • the push address your browser gives it, and the keys to encrypt to your browser,
  • your device's time zone, the reminder times you picked and which weekdays have anything due,
  • the last day the app said was done, so that day's evening check is skipped.

Nothing else: no name, no email, no goals, and not what any reminder says. A reminder only tells your device which one it is (morning or evening) and the date, encrypted so only your browser can read it. Your device then shows a text the app keeps on it. The server keeps no logs. It sees the IP address your device connects from when the app talks to it, as any website does, but doesn't keep it.

The reminder is delivered by the push service of your browser's maker: Apple (Safari, and the Home Screen app on iPhone and iPad), Google (Chrome, and most other browsers), Mozilla (Firefox) or Microsoft (Edge on Windows). That service sees that a message for your device came from the Arise server, when, and how big it was, but can't read it.

When you turn notifications off, the server deletes everything it had for that device. It also deletes it when the push service says the address is gone, for example because you removed the app or blocked its notifications, and when no reminder has got through to the device for two weeks.

Other services Arise uses

Each of these sees what any website sees when your device connects to it, like your IP address and the kind of device and browser. Each has its own privacy policy.

  • GitHub Pages hosts the website and the app. Your device fetches files from it when you open Arise and when Arise checks for updates: when you open it, when you come back to it, and every 30 minutes while it's open. The iPhone app checks a small file (latest.json) there the same way, and downloads new versions from there.
  • GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com) serves the exercise photos, from Free Exercise DB, when you open an exercise.
  • YouTube (Google) shows video thumbnails when you open an exercise, and plays the how-to videos when you play one. On the web they play in YouTube's privacy-enhanced player. In the iPhone app they open in the YouTube app or website.
  • Google Firebase runs accounts and sync, only if you make an account or sign in.
  • Tinfoil and Cloudflare run the private AI, only when you use it. Cloudflare also runs the free AI, only if you pick it.
  • Cloudflare also runs the reminders server, and the push service of your browser's maker (Apple, Google, Mozilla or Microsoft) delivers its reminders, only if you turn on notifications in the web app (see Reminders on the web).
  • The AI company you pick, only if you use your own AI service.

The app's fonts are part of the app, so loading them contacts nobody.

What Arise doesn't do

  • No ads.
  • No analytics, no trackers, and no cookies of its own.
  • We never sell your data, and we don't give it to anyone. The services above only get what this page says they get. With a recovery code account, we can't even read your synced data ourselves. With Email reset we could, but we don't.

Your choices

  • See and take your data. It's all in the app. Settings, Backup saves all of it to a file you can keep or load elsewhere.
  • Change it. Edit or delete anything in the app. With an account, the change syncs.
  • Erase it from a device. Settings, Danger zone, Erase all data. If you're signed in, this also signs you out and your encrypted cloud copy stays.
  • Delete your account. Settings, Account, More, then Delete my account and cloud copy. This needs your password. If you've forgotten it, set a new one first, with your recovery code or (with Email reset) a reset email. It deletes your encrypted cloud copy, your keys (with any copy kept for Email reset), the recovery record and your sign-in account. The data on your device stays until you erase it.
  • Sign out. Settings, Account. You can keep the data on the device, or remove it with Sign out and remove my data from this device (under More).
  • Stop the AI. Remove your AI key, pick a private option, or turn off the daily message, in Settings, AI coach.
  • Stop reminders. Turn off Notifications on this device in Settings, Reminders. The reminders server deletes what it had for that device. Erase all data turns them off too.

We don't read, hand over, change or restore your synced data for you. With a recovery code account we can't, because only your devices can open it. You can do all of that yourself in the app, as long as you can sign in.

Can't sign in any more? If you've lost both your password and your recovery code, we can still delete your account for you: your sign-in account, your encrypted cloud copy and the recovery record. See Contact for how to ask.

Children

Arise isn't meant for children under 13. It doesn't ask anyone's age, and we can't read what people put in it. If a child under 13 has made an account, they or a parent can delete it in Settings, Account, More, with its password. If neither of you has the password or the recovery code, ask us to delete it (see Contact).

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. Every change is also in the project's history on GitHub.

Contact

Questions or worries about privacy? Open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public, so don't put your email, account code, recovery code or anything else private in it.

To ask us to delete an account you can't sign in to, open an issue that says just that, with nothing private in it. We'll reply there with a private way to send us the account's email or account code. For an email account, we'll ask you to write from that address. Then we delete it from Firebase: the sign-in account, the encrypted cloud copy and the recovery record.
