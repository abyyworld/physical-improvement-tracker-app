Arise

Privacy policy

Last updated: 9 October 2026

Arise is a free app for daily quests toward your goals, with an optional workout plan and an AI coach. This page says what Arise keeps, where it's kept, and who can see it.

The short version

  • Everything you put in Arise is saved on your device.
  • An account is optional. With one, a copy is kept in the cloud so your devices stay in step. It's end-to-end encrypted: nobody else can read it, including the people who run Arise.
  • The AI coach is private by default. Only if you pick your own AI service, and say yes, does your data go to that company.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking. Your data is never sold.

Who runs Arise

Arise is a project on GitHub, run by abyyworld. Its code is public at github.com/abyyworld/physical-improvement-tracker-app, so anyone can check what this page says. "We" on this page means the people who run Arise.

This page covers the website (abyyworld.github.io/physical-improvement-tracker-app), the app you install from it on a phone or computer, and the iPhone app.

What's kept on your device

Everything you put in Arise is saved in the app's storage on your device: your goals, quests and ticks, measures and milestones, workouts, the daily log, weigh-ins and body measurements, your answers from the intro, your settings, and your chats and messages with the AI coach.

It isn't encrypted on the device itself. Anyone who can unlock your device and open Arise can see it.

Without an account, none of this leaves your device, unless you send it somewhere yourself: by saving a backup, or by using your own AI service (see The AI coach).

  • Backups. Settings, Backup saves everything to a file. The file is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.
  • Your AI key, if you add one, stays on that device. It's never synced and never put in backups.
  • The iPhone app also keeps a copy of your data in the Files app (On My iPhone, Arise), so it can put your data back if iOS ever clears the app's storage. Deleting the app deletes it. Like any app's data, it can be part of your iPhone's own backups (iCloud or a computer).
  • Reminders. The iPhone app's notifications are set up on the phone itself; no server sends them. On the web, "Add reminders to my calendar" saves a calendar file on your device. If you add it to a calendar that syncs (Google, iCloud and so on), that calendar service keeps those events.

Accounts and sync

An account is optional. It keeps the same history on every device where you sign in. You can make one with an email address, or with no email at all: then you get an account code (like ARISE-7KQ2-9XMP-4HTR-3C8D) to sign in with. Accounts and the cloud copy are run on Google Firebase. Without an account, Arise doesn't contact Firebase at all.

End-to-end encrypted

Before your data leaves your device, it's encrypted there (AES-256-GCM) with a random key that only your devices have. The cloud only ever holds the encrypted copy, and the database rules refuse anything that isn't encrypted. Nobody else can read it: not the people who run Arise, not Google, and not anyone who asks either of them for it.

Accounts made before Arise 2.0 kept a cloud copy that wasn't encrypted. It's replaced by an encrypted one the first time you enter your password in Arise 2.0 or later.

What syncs: everything listed above, except your AI key, which AI service you use, the notification switch, AI usage counts and a workout in progress. Those stay on each device.

Your password

Your password never leaves your device. The app stretches it on your device (PBKDF2, 600,000 rounds) into two separate keys. One is what the sign-in service gets in place of your password. The other unlocks the key to your data. The sign-in service can't turn what it gets back into either.

That's why nobody can reset your password for you. If you forget it, your recovery code is the only way back in.

One exception: an account made before Arise 2.0, or one whose password was set from a reset email, has that plain password at the sign-in service. That password is sent once, the way the old version did, and the account is then switched over. This only happens if you tick the box for it when signing in, or when you unlock a device that the old version had signed in.

Your recovery code

When you make an account, Arise shows you a recovery code once. Keep it somewhere safe and private. It also unlocks your data, so with it you can set a new password. We never see it and can't send it to you again. If you lose both your password and your recovery code, nobody can open your cloud copy, including us. The data on your devices stays.

For the recovery code to work, a small record is kept in the cloud under your sign-in email (or account code). It's locked with your recovery code, so it's no use to anyone without it. But someone who knows your sign-in email could find out that it has an Arise account.

What the server can still see

  • Your sign-in email. With a no-email account, only your account code.
  • When your devices sync, and roughly how much data you have (the size of the encrypted copy).
  • The IP address your device connects from, as with any website.

Not what any of your data says.

Arise doesn't send you emails. The only one you can get is a password reset link, which Firebase sends. The app only offers one for an account made before Arise 2.0. But Firebase sends one for any email account if someone asks it to, so you could get one you didn't ask for. You can ignore it. A password set that way can't open your data without your recovery code.

The AI coach

The AI coach is optional. Everything else in Arise works without it. To help you, it's given your profile and intro answers, your goals and quests and how they've gone, your workouts, weigh-ins and daily log, and what you type to it. You pick where it runs in Settings, AI coach.

Private AI

An open AI model running in secure enclaves at Tinfoil. Before anything is sent, the app checks that the enclave is running the exact published code on genuine secure hardware, then encrypts what it sends so only that enclave can read it. The answer comes back encrypted too.

Requests pass through a small Arise service on Cloudflare, which holds Arise's Tinfoil key and limits how much each account can use. It can't read what you ask or what the AI answers. It sees which account asked, from which IP address, when, and how big the request was. To know which account asked, it gets your sign-in token, which includes your sign-in email (or account code). It keeps no logs. All it keeps is one counter per account for the day's allowance, deleted at midnight UTC. Tinfoil sees that a request came from Arise, when, and how big it was. Your device also fetches the enclave's proof from Tinfoil to check it, so Tinfoil sees your IP address then.

The private AI needs an account. It's still being set up, so your version of Arise may not offer it yet.

On this device

Chrome's built-in AI model on laptops and desktops (Chrome 148 or later). What you ask never leaves your computer.

Your own AI service

You can connect your own key from Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google), OpenAI, OpenRouter, Groq, or another service you add. This is not private. The app sends your data straight from your device to that company, which can read it and handles it under its own privacy policy. We don't see it. The app only does this after you pick that service and say yes to it.

The daily message

When the AI coach is set up, it writes you a personal message once a day when you open the Today screen, using the same data. It's on unless you turn it off. With your own AI service, that means your data goes to that company once a day, even if you don't tap anything else. Turn it off in Settings, AI coach, Daily System message.

Other services Arise uses

Each of these sees what any website sees when your device connects to it, like your IP address and the kind of device and browser. Each has its own privacy policy.

  • GitHub Pages hosts the website and the app. Your device fetches files from it when you open Arise and when Arise checks for updates: when you open it, when you come back to it, and every 30 minutes while it's open. The iPhone app checks a small file (latest.json) there the same way, and downloads new versions from there.
  • GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com) serves the exercise photos, from Free Exercise DB, when you open an exercise.
  • YouTube (Google) shows video thumbnails when you open an exercise, and plays the how-to videos when you play one. On the web they play in YouTube's privacy-enhanced player. In the iPhone app they open in the YouTube app or website.
  • Google Firebase runs accounts and sync, only if you make an account or sign in.
  • Tinfoil and Cloudflare run the private AI, only when you use it.
  • The AI company you pick, only if you use your own AI service.

The app's fonts are part of the app, so loading them contacts nobody.

What Arise doesn't do

  • No ads.
  • No analytics, no trackers, and no cookies of its own.
  • We never sell your data, and we don't give it to anyone. The services above only get what this page says they get. We can't even read your synced data ourselves.

Your choices

  • See and take your data. It's all in the app. Settings, Backup saves all of it to a file you can keep or load elsewhere.
  • Change it. Edit or delete anything in the app. With an account, the change syncs.
  • Erase it from a device. Settings, Danger zone, Erase all data. If you're signed in, this also signs you out and your encrypted cloud copy stays.
  • Delete your account. Settings, Account, More, then Delete my account and cloud copy. This needs your password. If you've forgotten it, set a new one with your recovery code first. It deletes your encrypted cloud copy, your keys, the recovery record and your sign-in account. The data on your device stays until you erase it.
  • Sign out. Settings, Account. You can keep the data on the device, or remove it with Sign out and remove my data from this device (under More).
  • Stop the AI. Remove your AI key, pick a private option, or turn off the daily message, in Settings, AI coach.

We can't read, hand over, change or restore your synced data for you, because only your devices can open it. You can do all of that yourself in the app, as long as you can sign in.

Can't sign in any more? If you've lost both your password and your recovery code, we can still delete your account for you: your sign-in account, your encrypted cloud copy and the recovery record. See Contact for how to ask.

Children

Arise isn't meant for children under 13. It doesn't ask anyone's age, and we can't read what people put in it. If a child under 13 has made an account, they or a parent can delete it in Settings, Account, More, with its password. If neither of you has the password or the recovery code, ask us to delete it (see Contact).

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. Every change is also in the project's history on GitHub.

Contact

Questions or worries about privacy? Open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public, so don't put your email, account code, recovery code or anything else private in it.

To ask us to delete an account you can't sign in to, open an issue that says just that, with nothing private in it. We'll reply there with a private way to send us the account's email or account code. For an email account, we'll ask you to write from that address. Then we delete it from Firebase: the sign-in account, the encrypted cloud copy and the recovery record.
